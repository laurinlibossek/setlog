Privacy policy

Increment, the workout log for iPhone and Android, and Setlog, the same app for the web. Last updated 3 November 2026.

In short: Increment collects nothing about you. There's no account, no server, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. What you enter stays on your phone, and the developer never sees it.

Who is responsible

Laurin Libossek
github.com/laurinlibossek/setlog/issues

What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter: workouts, templates, exercises, notes, body weight and measurements, your settings, and the name you can enter in Settings. All of it is saved in the app's own storage on your phone. The app doesn't send any of it anywhere; there's no server it could go to.

Body weight and measurements are health data. They stay on your phone like everything else.

If your phone's own backup is on (iCloud Backup on iPhone, Google backup on Android), it can include the app's data. Apple or Google then store it under their privacy policies.

To delete everything, use Settings → Delete all data, or delete the app.

Internet

The app doesn't send anything over the internet and works fully offline.

What you send yourself

  • Backups and CSV exports are only made when you tap Back up now or Export as CSV. Your phone's share sheet opens, and you choose where the file goes (Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, AirDrop, email). From then on, the service you chose handles it.
  • Shared templates: Share on a template makes a link with that template's name, notes, exercises and sets, nothing else. You choose who gets it. The template is in the part of the link after "#", which browsers don't send to any server. Whoever opens the link in a browser loads the Setlog web app from GitHub Pages (see "The Setlog web app" below).
  • Imports: the app only reads files you pick yourself: a CSV export from another workout app, or a backup to restore.

Permissions

  • Notifications, only for the rest timer: when a rest is over and the app isn't open, a notification arrives. The phone schedules it itself; no push service is involved. The app asks the first time a rest timer starts. You can turn it off under Settings → Rest timer alerts, or in your phone's settings.
  • The app doesn't use the camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos, Apple Health or Health Connect.

Apple and Google

You get the app from the App Store or Google Play. Apple and Google process data about that, such as your account and the download, under their own privacy policies. If you've allowed it in your phone's settings (on iPhone: Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → Share With App Developers), they show the developer anonymous statistics such as the number of installs and crash reports. These don't include your workouts or anything else you entered in the app.

The Setlog web app

Setlog at laurinlibossek.github.io/setlog is the same app in the browser. It keeps everything in your browser's storage on your device, in the same way: no account, nothing uploaded.

It's hosted on GitHub Pages, as is this page. When your browser loads them, GitHub receives your IP address and the usual request data, like any website does (GitHub's privacy statement). Once installed, the web app loads from your device, and the browser only asks GitHub whether there's a new version.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct and delete your data, to restrict its use, to take it with you, and to object, and you can complain to a data protection authority. The developer has no data about you, so there's nothing on the developer's side to hand over or delete. Your data is on your phone, where you can export it (Back up now, Export as CSV) and delete it (Delete all data) at any time.
